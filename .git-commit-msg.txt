Flip the AI tutor and flashcard suggestions to English

Two surfaces still running Hakiya's direction. Neither errored, and neither
could: an assistant that fluently answers the wrong question reads exactly
like an assistant that answers the right one.

assistant-chat is the Ask AI panel, reachable from every page. It
introduced itself as "a friendly expert in spoken Gulf/Egyptian/Yemeni",
answered in English because the learner was assumed to be an English
speaker, and asked for Latin transliterations of Arabic words. It is now an
English teacher whose learners are native Arabic speakers, and it explains
in the learner's own dialect — a learner whose English is too weak to read
English learns nothing from an explanation they have to decode first. The
English being discussed is quoted unchanged in Latin script, because it is
the material; hard words are followed by their pronunciation in Arabic
letters, the way the rest of the app writes them. It runs through the Brain
with target: 'english', so it gets the L1-interference rules and can name
the Arabic habit behind a predictable mistake.

The seed was the sharper problem. Every caller sends the pair the same way
round it always did — english is the target line, arabic its gloss — but
the prompt labelled the ARABIC as "THE SENTENCE" and the English as
"translation provided". A chat opened from an English story line was
therefore told the gloss was the thing being asked about. Seeds with no
English half still work: bridged Hakiya clips are Arabic all the way down,
and dropping the seed there would un-ground the chat on exactly the content
where a learner is most likely lost. AskAiContextCard follows the same
order — English leads, gloss underneath, and collapsing keeps the line in
the header rather than the gloss.

suggest-flashcards generated dialect Arabic words for a topic with English
as the translation, returned a Latin transliteration, and deduplicated on
the Arabic. That last one was a real defect: post-flip the Arabic is the
gloss, so a learner who already owns "big" was silently refused "large" —
both gloss to كبير, so the second was filtered as a duplicate — by the one
feature whose entire job is finding words they do not have. It now
generates English, glosses it in the learner's dialect, returns phonetic_ar
instead of a romanization, and dedupes on the English. normalize() was
folding ta marbuta and the alif family, which is what Arabic near-duplicates
differ by; English's differ by punctuation and spacing, so it folds that
instead ("Check-in." matches "check in"). The dialog sends English
headwords, leads each card with the English, and its chrome is Arabic — it
was still saying "2 selected" and "Added 2 words" in an Arabic-first UI.

Tests pin the direction rather than the output, since both directions
produce well-formed results: the prompt assertions read which half is
labelled the sentence, and the dedupe cases use a synonym pair that only
survives if the filter compares the English.
